## 1. Why this task existed

TASK 08 established a clean fine-grained transfer protocol (val carved from the official train split, checkpoint selected on val, official test split touched exactly once) and got a **dataset-dependent** answer: SAGA was BELOW baseline on CUB ViT-S and ABOVE on Aircraft ViT-S, both clearing 2xSE, while the two ViT-B cells had a single ft-seed each and so could carry no error bar at all.

TASK 13 had three sub-goals, in the task file's own priority order:

1. **Seed fill** — add ft-seeds 1 and 2 to the four ViT-B cells so their deltas can carry an SE. (Highest value per GPU-minute.)
2. **Ring ablation** — an eval-only test of *why* the datasets differ, run on the checkpoints TASK 08 had already produced.
3. **A third dataset** — only if one was already staged on the HPC.

The hypothesis under test, stated in the task file and **not assumed true**: *positional suppression helps when the border region is uninformative for the class (planes against sky) and hurts when it carries class evidence (birds in habitat).* TASK 07 supplied the coordinates: sinks, and SAGA's strongest gate suppression, sit on **ring 1** — one patch inside the border of the 14x14 grid, not on the outermost ring.

### 2.2 Ring ablation — the mechanism test

Four input conditions per fine-tuned checkpoint, all applied in PIXEL space to patch regions of the 14x14 grid, so no model surgery is involved:

| condition | what is masked | role |
|---|---|---|
| `full` | nothing | must reproduce the committed `test_final.json` |
| `mask_ring1` | the 44 ring-1 patch regions | the hypothesis |
| `mask_center44` | the 44 most central regions | area-matched control |
| `mask_random44` | 44 seeded random regions | position-agnostic control |

Masked pixels are replaced by the dataset's own per-channel mean, computed over its TRAIN split (never the test split), post-resize and pre-normalize.

**The quantity that carries the result is the CONTRAST** `drop_ring1 - drop_random44`, which collapses algebraically to `top1_random44 - top1_ring1`. Both terms come from the SAME run, the SAME checkpoint and the SAME test images, and the two masks are area-matched at 44 patches — so class count, train-set size, backbone quality and *how much any masking costs at all* cancel out of it. A raw drop has none of those properties, which is why the hypothesis is never read off one.

**Stated prediction:** a LARGER ring-1 drop for CUB than for Aircraft, relative to each dataset's own `random44` control — i.e. a HIGHER contrast for CUB.

| dataset | n runs | drop ring1 | drop center44 | drop random44 | **CONTRAST** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CUB | 12 | +1.124 ± 0.135 | +23.112 ± 0.253 | +3.302 ± 0.112 | **-2.179 ± 0.137** |
| Aircraft | 12 | +1.920 ± 0.126 | +26.742 ± 0.743 | +9.141 ± 0.442 | **-7.221 ± 0.368** |

**Verdict: the predicted ORDERING HOLDS.** CUB -2.179 against Aircraft -7.221; difference **+5.0418** (se=0.3928; |diff|-2se=+4.2562; significant_2xSE=1; UNPAIRED (different images and models); prediction: CUB > Aircraft).

The ordering is not an artefact of pooling: per cell, CUB spans -2.462..-1.571 and Aircraft spans -8.531..-5.450 — **disjoint**, with a gap of 2.988 between the two sets.

### 2.3 The three qualifications that bound that verdict

These are not caveats added for politeness; they are forced by the same table and they change what the result can be used to claim.

1. **Both contrasts are NEGATIVE** (-2.179 and -7.221). Masking ring 1 costs LESS than masking 44 random patches on BOTH datasets — so ring 1 is *less* informative than an average area-matched region everywhere, CUB included. The hypothesis's ordinal prediction survives; its mechanism story — that CUB's border *carries class evidence* — is **NOT supported in absolute terms**.
2. **The raw ring-1 drop orders the OTHER way**: Aircraft +1.920 against CUB +1.124 — masking ring 1 costs Aircraft MORE in absolute points. The contrast reverses that only because Aircraft's `random44` control is much larger (+9.141 against +3.302, a factor of 2.77). **The result is carried by the denominator** — how much a random region matters — not by ring 1 itself.
3. **The centre is where the evidence is, on both datasets.** `mask_center44` costs +23.112 (CUB) and +26.742 (Aircraft) for the same 44 patches — an order of magnitude more than ring 1. Both datasets are object-centred and no border story competes with that.

### 2.4 The validity control

The `full` condition re-runs the TASK-08 eval path unmodified and must reproduce each run's committed `test_final.json` top-1 within 0.05.

**24 of 24 pass, and the largest absolute deviation across all 24 runs is 0.000 points.** Every run reproduces its committed number EXACTLY — so the ablation's baseline is the committed number itself, not an approximation of it. Consistency across the 24 files: 1 distinct seed, 1 distinct grid side, and 1 distinct ring-1 index set (1 = the TASK-07 definition was applied identically everywhere).

## 3. What was NOT done, and why

### 3.1 The third dataset (sub-goal 3) — SKIPPED

Stanford Cars and Oxford Flowers-102 were **not** added, and no dataset was downloaded. The human confirmed on 2026-09-15 that neither is staged on the HPC and declined the sub-goal; the task file made it conditional on exactly that. The supporting reasoning, recorded at the time:

- A third dataset differs from CUB and Aircraft along class count, train size and fine-grained axis **simultaneously**, so it would have contributed a third confounded point rather than isolating background informativeness.
- Neither candidate is a clean *a priori* test case — cars sit on roads and in showrooms, flowers sit in foliage — so either outcome could have been narrated after the fact.
- The ring ablation isolates the same variable **within** each dataset, at a fixed checkpoint and test set, which is a stronger design than adding a third correlational point.
- It was also the largest engineering item in the task (two dataset parsers, a split builder, two committed split files, tests, plus 6 fine-tune runs) for the weakest inference.

**Consequence, stated plainly: no claim of generalization beyond CUB and Aircraft is available, and none is made anywhere in this task.** `tests/test_task13_ring_ablation.py::test_third_dataset_not_configured_until_a_path_is_confirmed` pins the matrix to {cub, aircraft}, so a dataset cannot be added later without its split builder, its committed split file and its own tests.

### 3.2 Deliberately out of scope

- **The TASK-08 trainer and protocol were not touched.** The seed fill adds config rows only; the test split is still touched exactly once per training run. The ring ablation is eval-only: it trains nothing, writes no checkpoint, and selects nothing — the checkpoint is frozen and was already chosen on val, and its sha256 is asserted against the one recorded in `test_final.json`.
- **The ring definition was not re-derived.** It is imported from TASK-07's `analysis/address_analysis.py`. The distance map was factored out of `border_rings` into `border_distance_map()` / `ring_indices()`; a test recomputes `border_rings` from an inline copy of the pre-refactor body to prove the extraction changed nothing.
- **No figure was produced.** The task file asked for two tables and a note; it did not ask for a figure, and none is needed to read a four-condition contrast.

## 7. Decisions a reviewer should know about

| decision | why | where it is recorded |
|---|---|---|
| `mask_center44` needs a tie-break | the concentric rings of a 14x14 grid hold 4, 12, 20, 28 positions counting inwards, so NO union of whole rings equals 44 (4+12+20 = 36; the next overshoots to 64) | order is border-distance DESC, then Euclidean-from-centre ASC, then index; the 44 indices are written into every output JSON |
| the fill is the dataset's own mean, from the TRAIN split | "the dataset's per-channel mean" is ambiguous between the ImageNet normalization mean and the dataset's own; using the train split keeps "test touched once" airtight | `fill_source` and `fill_n_images` in every JSON |
| matrix rows APPENDED, never inserted | `gen_ft_jobs.py` maps array index -> run by POSITION; inserting would silently re-map TASK-08's committed indices 0-15 | comment in `configs/ft_matrix.yaml` |
| the ablation job runs on a100, not a40 | the human's instruction; `--cpus-per-task` moved 4 -> 8 with it, because the 4 belonged to the a40 precedent and cpus must match the partition they were measured on | a test pins all four SLURM values EQUAL to the committed ft a100 header |
| TASK-08's `finegrained.md` was regenerated | it is DERIVED from T3, and after the seed fill it was left asserting ViT-B "n = 1 => no significance claim is possible" while its own source table said n=3 | generator unchanged; only the ViT-B rows moved |

## 9. Open items — what the next person should consider

1. **Both ViT-B margins are thin.** CUB ViT-B clears 2xSE by only +0.021 on n=3. A fourth and fifth ft-seed (`ft_seed: 3, 4` appended to the matrix, then `--array=24-31`) would settle whether either is stable.
2. **The contrast is carried by the `random44` denominator**, not by ring 1 itself (§2.3 item 2). A control that varies the mask POSITION at matched control difficulty would separate those two explanations. No such control exists in this task.
3. **`mask_random44` uses a single seed (0) for every run**, so the control is one draw rather than an average over draws. Re-running `tools/ring_ablation.py --seed <k>` for a few k would put an error bar on the control itself; the tool already records the seed and refuses to treat a different seed as a no-op.
4. **The CUB ViT-S / ViT-B sign disagreement is unexplained.** The two CUB SAGA cells do differ in ring contrast, but with one contrast value per cell there is no way to test whether that difference drives the sign flip.
5. **The legacy fine-grained numbers remain VOID.** The legacy e6 results — **+2.19 Aircraft and +1.29 CUB** — came from a protocol that used the official TEST split as its validation set and selected `best.pth` on it (bug B7). They measure a different and invalid quantity, they are never cited, and **they must not be compared against anything in this handoff.**
